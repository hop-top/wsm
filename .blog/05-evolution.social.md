## Facebook Groups

### Variant 1
The quarterly topology audit:

□ Any collaborations >1 month old? → Force graduation
□ Any queues >10 deep? → Add capacity or convert to platform
□ Any enabling squads >6 months? → Audit exit condition
□ Reclaim rates >10%? → Reduce oversight
□ Shadow platforms emerged? → Fix official platform

Evolution isn't optional. It's the difference between a topology that serves you and one that constrains you.

### Variant 2
The graduation rules for interaction modes:

Collaboration → X-as-a-Service:
When the interface is stable. Remove scheduled syncs. Provider treats requests as anonymous.

Facilitating → Independence:
When learner can operate without facilitator. No more teaching sessions.

Supervised-Delegation → X-as-a-Service:
When executor demonstrates reliability. Remove checkpoints.

### Variant 3
Evolution anti-patterns:

• Evolution by crisis (only change when everything is on fire)
• Evolution by HIPPO (highest paid person's opinion)
• Evolution never (squads.yaml is sacred)
• Evolution too often (weekly changes, no stability)

## Threads

### Variant 1
Topologies drift because:

• New capabilities emerge
• Workloads shift
• Collaboration overstays
• Expertise concentrates
• Platforms decay

The topology that was correct becomes incorrect. Not because you were wrong. Because the world changed.

### Variant 2
The bottleneck squad signal:

A complicated-subsystem has a queue. Requests take days. Other squads work around them.

What it means: Mis-typed or under-resourced.

Fixes:
- Repetitive/high-volume → convert to platform
- Genuine expertise → add capacity or split
- Poor interface → fix the interface

### Variant 3
Evolution isn't one-and-done.

It's continuous. Measure → identify → diagnose → design → implement → iterate.

The best topologies are the ones that change.

## LinkedIn Article

**Title: Evolution: When and How to Reshape Your Agent Topology**

Your topology is alive. Or it's dead.

Alive means it changes. Squads get added. Interactions shift. Collaboration graduates to services. The shape adapts to reality.

Dead means it's frozen. The squads.yaml file was written once and never touched. New work gets shoehorned into old structures. No one remembers why the topology was designed this way.

Evolution isn't optional. It's the difference between a topology that serves you and one that constrains you.

**Signals that evolution is needed:**
- Collaboration never graduated (>1 month old)
- Bottleneck squads (queue > threshold)
- Shadow platforms emerging
- Enabling squads >6 months old
- Orchestrator involved in every decision

**The evolution process:**
1. Measure — interaction frequency, queue depth, reclaim rate
2. Identify drift — compare measurements to expectations
3. Diagnose root cause — is the interface broken? squad mis-typed?
4. Design the evolution — add/remove/change?
5. Implement and monitor — update squads.yaml, track metrics
6. Iterate — evolution is continuous
